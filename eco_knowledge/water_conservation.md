# Water Conservation Guide

## Why Water Conservation Matters
- Only 3% of Earth's water is fresh water, and two-thirds is locked in glaciers and ice caps.
- Water treatment and pumping accounts for ~1% of global electricity use — saving water saves energy.
- By 2025, two-thirds of the world could face water shortages (UN Water).
- In the UK, average daily household water use is 150 litres per person.

## In the Bathroom
- A standard shower uses 8–15 litres per minute; a bath uses 80 litres on average.
- A 5-minute shower uses 40–75 litres; cutting shower time to 4 minutes saves 5,000 litres per year.
- Turn off the tap while brushing teeth — saves 12 litres per minute.
- Install a low-flow showerhead (under £20) and reduce water use by 40% with no noticeable difference.
- Fix a dripping tap — one dripping tap wastes up to 15 litres per day (5,000 litres/year).
- Install a dual-flush toilet or a toilet cistern bag to reduce flush volume.

## In the Kitchen
- Only boil as much water as you need in the kettle — saves energy too.
- Wash fruit and vegetables in a bowl, not under a running tap. Reuse the water for plants.
- Run the dishwasher only when full on eco mode — uses less water than hand-washing.
- Defrost food in the fridge overnight, not under a running tap.
- Keep a jug of water in the fridge so you don't run the tap waiting for cold water.

## Garden and Outdoor
- Water plants in early morning or evening to minimise evaporation — saves 30% more water.
- Install a water butt/rain barrel to collect rainwater for garden use — free and chlorine-free.
- A water butt can collect 24,000 litres per year from an average roof.
- Use a watering can instead of a hosepipe — a hosepipe uses 1,000 litres per hour.
- Choose drought-resistant native plants for your garden.
- Mulch plant beds to retain soil moisture and reduce watering frequency by 50%.

## Greywater Recycling
- Greywater (from sinks, showers, and washing machines) can be reused for toilet flushing and garden irrigation.
- A domestic greywater system can save up to 50 litres per person per day.
- Simple gravity-fed systems cost £200–£500 and require no energy to run.

## Rainwater Harvesting
- Collect and store rainwater from roofs via gutters and downpipes into tanks.
- Can supply up to 50% of non-potable water needs (toilets, laundry, garden).
- In India, rainwater harvesting is mandated in many states for new buildings over 100 sq m.

## Impact Statistics
- A household with a water meter uses 10–15% less water than one without.
- Agriculture accounts for 70% of global freshwater withdrawals — eating lower on the food chain also saves water.
- Producing 1 kg of beef requires 15,000 litres of water; 1 kg of wheat requires 1,500 litres.
- A 10-minute shower uses 80–150 litres — same as several days of drinking water for someone in a water-scarce region.

## Today's Eco-Action
Time your next shower and challenge yourself to finish in under 4 minutes. Put a timer on your phone — this simple change saves up to 12,000 litres of water per year.
