# Composting and Waste Reduction Guide

## Why Compost?
- Food and garden waste in landfill produces methane — a greenhouse gas 84x more potent than CO₂ over 20 years.
- Composting diverts organic waste from landfill, reducing methane emissions significantly.
- Finished compost improves soil structure, reduces need for synthetic fertilisers, and increases plant yield.
- The average UK household throws away 6.6 million tonnes of food waste per year.

## How to Start Home Composting
1. Choose a compost bin or designate a compost heap in a corner of your garden.
2. Layer "greens" (nitrogen-rich: fruit/vegetable peelings, grass clippings, coffee grounds, tea bags) with "browns" (carbon-rich: cardboard, paper, dry leaves, wood chips) in roughly 1:3 ratio.
3. Keep the pile moist but not waterlogged — it should feel like a wrung-out sponge.
4. Turn the pile every 2–4 weeks to introduce oxygen and speed decomposition.
5. Compost is ready in 2–6 months when it's dark, crumbly, and smells earthy.

## What to Compost (Greens & Browns)
**Yes — Greens (nitrogen):**
- Fruit and vegetable peelings and scraps
- Coffee grounds and paper coffee filters
- Tea leaves and bags (remove staples)
- Fresh grass clippings
- Plate scrapings (no meat, fish, or dairy in open heaps)

**Yes — Browns (carbon):**
- Cardboard (torn into small pieces)
- Paper bags, newspaper, egg boxes
- Dry autumn leaves
- Woody prunings (shredded)
- Straw and hay

**No — avoid these:**
- Meat, fish, and cooked food (attracts rats in open bins)
- Dairy products
- Diseased plants
- Pet waste
- Coal or coke ash

## Worm Composting (Vermicomposting)
- Worm bins work in small spaces including flats and apartments.
- Red wigglers (Eisenia fetida) process food scraps 2–3x faster than traditional composting.
- Worm castings are among the most nutrient-dense natural fertilisers available.
- A worm bin can process up to 3 kg of food waste per week.

## Municipal Food Waste Collection
- Many councils now provide brown/organic bins for food and garden waste.
- Use a lidded kitchen caddy to collect scraps before transferring to the outdoor bin.
- Line the caddy with compostable bags or newspaper to reduce mess.
- Commercial compost facilities can process cooked food, meat, and dairy — unlike home heaps.

## Zero-Waste Shopping
- Bring your own containers to bulk food shops and delis.
- Refuse unnecessary packaging at checkout — speak to store managers about plastic-free options.
- Shop at farmers' markets and zero-waste stores for package-free produce.
- Choose concentrated cleaning products — less packaging per use.

## Impact Statistics
- Composting all UK food waste would save the equivalent of 1 in 5 cars off the road.
- 1 tonne of food waste composted instead of landfilled prevents 0.5–1 tonne of CO₂e.
- Home composting can save a household 150 kg of CO₂e per year.
