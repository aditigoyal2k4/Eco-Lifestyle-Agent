# Sustainable Fashion and Clothing Guide

## The Problem with Fast Fashion
- The fashion industry produces 10% of global CO₂ emissions — more than aviation and shipping combined.
- 85% of all textiles end up in landfill or are incinerated each year — equivalent to one rubbish truck per second.
- Producing one cotton T-shirt uses 2,700 litres of water — equivalent to 2.5 years of drinking water for one person.
- A single synthetic garment sheds up to 700,000 microfibres per wash, polluting waterways and oceans.

## The Second-Hand Revolution
- Buying second-hand extends the life of clothing and saves 82% of the energy used to produce a new garment.
- Charity shops, thrift stores, and apps like Vinted, Depop, and eBay make second-hand easy.
- Car boot sales, clothing swaps, and repair cafés are community alternatives.
- Vintage and second-hand clothing is increasingly fashionable — a £20 second-hand find is a talking point, not a compromise.

## How to Build a Sustainable Wardrobe
- Buy less, choose well, make it last (Vivienne Westwood's mantra).
- Invest in quality basics in neutral colours that mix and match easily.
- Choose natural fibres where possible: organic cotton, linen, wool, hemp, and Tencel (lyocell).
- Avoid polyester, nylon, and acrylic where possible — they shed microfibres and don't biodegrade.
- Use a Guppyfriend wash bag to catch microfibres from synthetic clothing.

## Sustainable Brands and Certifications
- GOTS (Global Organic Textile Standard) — certifies organic fibre production from field to final product.
- Fair Trade — ensures fair wages and safe working conditions for garment workers.
- B Corp certification — companies meeting high standards for social and environmental performance.
- Look for brands like Patagonia, Eileen Fisher, Thought Clothing, and People Tree.

## Caring for Your Clothes to Make Them Last
- Wash clothes at 30°C to preserve fibres and reduce energy use.
- Air dry instead of tumble drying — tumble dryers account for 4% of household energy use.
- Learn basic repairs: sewing on a button, patching a hole, replacing a zip. YouTube has tutorials for everything.
- Store clothes properly — fold knitwear, hang structured garments, keep moths away with cedar blocks.
- Dry clean only when absolutely necessary — chemicals are harsh on fibres and the environment.

## Clothing Disposal
- Donate wearable items to charity shops, shelters, or clothing banks.
- Worn or damaged textiles can go to H&M, Zara, and other retailer take-back schemes.
- Some councils and supermarkets have textile recycling banks.
- Never put textiles in your general waste bin — even worn-out clothing can be recycled into industrial rags and insulation.

## Impact Statistics
- The average person buys 60% more clothing than 15 years ago and keeps each item half as long.
- Extending the life of garments by just 9 months reduces carbon, water, and waste impacts by 20–30%.
- The secondhand clothing market is growing 3x faster than the broader apparel market.

## Today's Eco-Action
Look through your wardrobe and identify 3 items you haven't worn in the past year. Donate, swap, or sell them — and resist buying anything new for the next 30 days.
