# Comprehensive Recycling Guide

## The Recycling Hierarchy
1. **Reduce** — use less in the first place
2. **Reuse** — find a second life for items
3. **Recycle** — process materials into new products
4. **Recover** — energy recovery from waste
5. **Landfill** — last resort

## Plastics Recycling Guide

### Widely Accepted (Kerbside)
- **#1 PET** (clear bottles, fruit punnets) — rinse clean, remove cap
- **#2 HDPE** (milk bottles, shampoo bottles, cleaning product bottles) — rinse, keep cap on
- **#5 PP** (yoghurt pots, margarine tubs) — accepted in many areas, check locally

### Rarely Accepted Kerbside
- **#3 PVC** (cling film, some window frames) — take to specialist recycler
- **#4 LDPE** (soft plastic bags, bread bags) — supermarket soft plastic collection points
- **#6 PS** (polystyrene cups, packaging) — specialist recycler only
- **#7 Other** — generally not recyclable

### Black Plastic
- Black plastic is almost never recyclable kerbside — carbon black pigment blocks infrared sorting.
- Check if a brand offers take-back; otherwise avoid black plastic packaging.

## Paper and Cardboard
- **Yes:** newspapers, magazines, office paper, cardboard boxes (flattened), egg cartons, paper bags
- **No:** tissues and kitchen roll (contaminated), paper cups (plastic-lined), greasy pizza boxes (bottom half is contaminated — rip off the clean lid)
- Tip: if paper is wet, it goes to landfill — keep your recycling dry.

## Glass
- **Yes:** bottles and jars of any colour — rinse clean, lids off (recycle lids separately)
- **No:** Pyrex/oven-proof glass, drinking glasses, window glass, mirrors, light bulbs, ceramics — these contaminate glass batches
- Take non-bottle glass to a household waste recycling centre (HWRC).

## Metals
- **Yes:** aluminium cans, steel cans, tin foil (scrunched into a ball the size of a fist), aerosol cans (empty), metal lids
- Aluminium is infinitely recyclable — recycling aluminium uses 95% less energy than primary production.
- Tip: crush cans to save space in your bin.

## Electronic Waste (E-Waste)
- **Never** put electronics in general waste or recycling — contains toxic metals (lead, mercury, cadmium).
- Return to: manufacturer take-back programmes, retailer drop-offs (Currys, Best Buy), local HWRC e-waste area.
- Phones and tablets: donate if functional, recycle via manufacturer scheme or specialist (e.g. Mazuma, musicMagpie in UK).
- Batteries: collect in a lidded container and take to supermarket battery collection boxes.

## Textiles
- **Donate** wearable clothing to charity shops, clothing banks, or apps like Vinted and Depop.
- **Recycle** worn textiles at H&M, Zara, Marks & Spencer, or council textile banks.
- **Never** put textiles in your general waste or recycling bin.

## Hazardous Household Waste
- Paint, solvents, motor oil, pesticides — take to your local HWRC hazardous waste area.
- Medicines — return to any pharmacy for safe disposal (not down the sink or in the bin).
- Fluorescent light tubes and energy-saving bulbs — HWRC or Recolight collection points.

## Common Recycling Mistakes
1. Wishful recycling (putting non-recyclables in the bin hoping they'll be sorted) — contaminates whole batches.
2. Not rinsing containers — food residue contaminates recyclables.
3. Putting shredded paper loose in the bin — too small for sorting machines; bag it first.
4. Recycling wet materials — wet paper and cardboard cannot be recycled.
5. Bagging recyclables — don't put recyclables in plastic bags; loose is best.
